**Purpose**

Existing social impact assessments often produce numerical indicators that are difficult to interpret. This work develops a quantitative methodology, with notable similarity to techno-economic analysis (TEA) and life cycle assessment, that evaluates how engineering designs contribute to or detract from meeting global human needs in an efficient and practical way.


**Methods**

The proposed methodology builds on established social life cycle assessment principles and PSILCA indicators while introducing an explicit framework for interpreting the severity and direction of social impacts. Human needs are organized into three hierarchical categories: survival, functional society, and fulfillment. Social indicators are classified within this hierarchy using evidence from international organizations, human development objectives, and causes of human mortality. The social impacts are divided into scope 1 (direct) and scope 2 (embodied) impacts and analyzed over a 30-year production lifetime to calculate the Net Present Impact, in a manner analogous to the Net Present Value calculated by the TEA. 


**Results and Discussion**

The methodology was applied to multiple case studies in the energy, consumer products, sustenance, and defense sectors. The results show that the methodology does a reasonable job at representing the major effects of the designs on addressing the needs of the human population. The aspects of the methodology that may be considered arbitrary, market value and human need scale, are analyzed with sensitivity and scenario analysis to demonstrate that the methodology is a robust and valid framework.
